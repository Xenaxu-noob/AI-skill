# AI Explainer Video

Build a **60–90 second narrated explainer** in a consistent flat-vector / AI-plate hybrid style. The output is a finished master video plus the reusable script, storyboard, voiceover, captions, and source project.

This skill is for **explaining one complex thing clearly**, not for making a cinematic brand film.

## When to use

Use this skill when the request matches most of these:

- One abstract or technical idea must be understood in 60–90 seconds.
- There is a script, article, report, deck, or rough voiceover — but no footage.
- No shoot, actors, location, or animation team is available.
- The deliverable needs a hook, captions, SFX, and a clear CTA.
- The same master must become 9:16 / 1:1 cutdowns or multiple languages.
- The content is a series, not a one-off: consistent characters, palette, typography, transitions.
- The subject is AI, software, science, finance, policy, health literacy, education, or internal process.
- The budget is closer to motion graphics than to live action.

## When not to use

- Emotional brand film, lifestyle, fashion, luxury, comedy.
- Precise product UI walkthroughs — record the product instead.
- Live-action testimonials, founder talking-head, event coverage.
- Performance-led storytelling that depends on an actor's face.
- Regulated medical/financial claims that need legal review before visuals.
- 10+ minute technical courses; use a different pacing format.
- Cinema-grade 4K delivery; this pipeline is optimized for 1080p/2K.

## Output contract

Deliver:

- `master_16x9.mp4` — 1920×1080, 30 or 60 fps, 60–90 s.
- `master_9x16.mp4` and/or `master_1x1.mp4` — recomposed, not just cropped.
- `script.md` — final narration with timecodes and word count.
- `storyboard.csv` — shot list with scene, visual, on-screen text, VO, SFX, duration.
- `voiceover.wav` — final narration, 48 kHz.
- `captions.srt` — synced, line-broken for mobile.
- `thumbnail.jpg` — one readable frame, no clutter.
- Source project / renderer / assets.
- Optional: AI plates, character pack, prompt log, cost log.

## Core workflow

### 0. Fit check and brief

Collect:

- Audience: who must understand this, and what do they already know?
- One sentence they must repeat afterward.
- Desired action: visit, buy, book, download, share, internal approval.
- Duration: 45 / 60 / 90 s.
- Language(s), voice, subtitles.
- Claims that must not be exaggerated.
- Brand constraints: colors, fonts, logo, legal lines.
- Distribution: website, YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok/Reels, conference, sales email.

If the answer to “what is the one sentence?” is unclear, stop and write it before scripting.

### 1. Thesis and 10-beat structure

Write one sentence:

> “Most people think X, but actually Y — and here is why it matters.”

Then map to 10 beats:

1. Hook — question, contradiction, or cost of not knowing.
2. Stakes — why this is hard / why it matters.
3. Reveal — name the subject.
4. Proof — one concrete number or comparison.
5. Analogy — the central metaphor.
6. Mechanism — how it works, step by step.
7. Product / proof — what it does in practice.
8. Team / craft — who made it and why.
9. Check — one verification or caveat.
10. Close — thesis restated + CTA.

### 2. Script

Budget:

- 60 s ≈ 150–210 English words, or 220–300 Chinese characters.
- 90 s ≈ 230–300 English words, or 330–450 Chinese characters.
- Target ≈ 170–190 English wpm; leave 2–3 pauses for SFX/visual beats.
- One idea per sentence. No nested clauses.
- Write for the ear, not the page: short verbs, concrete nouns, no jargon without a plain-language swap.

Every 5–8 seconds, give the viewer a new visual event: object, label, cut, or motion.

### 3. Storyboard

- 20–30 shots for 60–90 s; average 2.2–3.2 s.
- Hard cuts for 80–90% of transitions.
- 4–6 white splash wipes at act changes only.
- One “special move” per film: a pullback, a match cut, a stack growth, or a reveal.
- Every shot must answer: what changes on screen?
- Text and narration must not repeat each other word for word; text is a label, VO is the argument.
- Hook must land by 0:03. CTA must be readable by 0:58.

### 4. Style bible

Define before generating assets:

- 3 characters: protagonist robot, human viewer, scientist/operator; optional team.
- Palette: 1 background family, 1 accent, 1 warning, 1 success.
- Typography: display Latin + CJK sans; 3 sizes only.
- Shapes: rounded rectangles, flat fills, no outlines.
- Camera: 2–4% drift; one larger move per film.
- Transitions: hard cut + white splash wipe.
- Sound: 1 music bed, 1 whoosh, 1 pop, 1 success chime.

Consistency beats novelty. Reuse the same character silhouettes, palette, and card style across the series.

### 5. AI background plates

Use AI only for **wide / exterior / interior backgrounds with empty foreground**, never for faces, hands, text, logos, or precise UI.

Plate prompt rules:

- Full frontal eye-level view; camera level; one-point perspective.
- Main surfaces parallel to the picture plane.
- Blank signs, blank screens, blank boards, blank posters.
- Empty floor / empty seats for later character placement.
- Backless stools or stools with no backrest; no high-back chairs.
- No people, no text, no letters, no logo, no watermark, no UI cards.
- 2048×1152 (16:9), 3 variants, select one.
- If the model adds people/text/UI, discard and retry; do not “fix it in post” unless the area can be covered cleanly.

Do not generate text inside the plate. All text is drawn in code or design software so it stays sharp and editable.

See `references/ai-plate-prompts.md`.

### 6. Code-drawn overlays

Draw characters, labels, cards, UI, and data in code (Canvas/HTML/SVG/After Effects) so they remain editable and perfectly sharp.

Rules:

- Text is axis-aligned to the screen, not pasted onto a perspective surface.
- If a plate surface is angled, either replace it with a flat code panel or move the text to a flat surface.
- Cards: text centered horizontally and vertically; 16–24 px inner padding; never let text touch the border.
- Keep 80 px safe margins; mobile 9:16 needs 140 px top/bottom.
- Character hands must be visible; phone screens face the character, so the audience sees the phone back.
- Shoes must cover the pant-leg ends; no dark cap peeking below the sole.
- Seated characters: body bottom on the seat; no floating above or sinking through.

### 7. Voice, music, SFX

- One voice, one accent, consistent pace.
- Generate a pronunciation guide for brand names and technical terms.
- Music: low-mid energy, no vocal, duck −12 to −15 dB under VO.
- SFX: whoosh on cuts, pop on labels, chime on success; do not stack more than 3 layers.
- Final mix: −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP (target −1.8).
- Captions: 1–2 lines, ≤ 14 CJK chars or ≤ 42 Latin chars per line; sync to phrase, not word.

### 8. Render and QA

Technical baseline:

- 1920×1080, 30 or 60 fps, H.264 CRF 16, yuv420p, BT.709.
- AAC 192 kbps, 48 kHz stereo.
- If using motion-blur subframes, composite with a running average, not 1/N alpha.
- Grade: subtle saturate/brightness; vignette 0.03–0.06; grain 0.10–0.14.
- No black frames, no freeze > 0.5 s.

QA checklist:

- All text inside its card, centered, no clipping.
- No text over a face, hand, or busy detail.
- No AI-generated gibberish text or UI cards.
- Characters have arms; phone is back-facing; shoes cover legs.
- Chairs are backless where specified; sitters are on the seat.
- Hook readable at 0:03; CTA readable at the end.
- Captions match VO; brand names pronounced correctly.
- 9:16 version recomposed, not cropped.
- Loudness and true peak verified.

See `references/qa-checklist.md`.

## Shot grammar

| Time | Beat | Typical visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Hook | One strong question, object, or contradiction |
| 3–8 s | Stakes | Three cards, pressure, cost, “every token/every voice” |
| 8–14 s | Reveal | Nameplate, spotlight, one hero object |
| 14–24 s | Proof | Building, street, lab, stage; scale and credibility |
| 24–34 s | Mechanism | Library / classroom / control room; how it works |
| 34–45 s | Product | Chat, desk, code, art studio |
| 45–55 s | Craft | Script, art, animation, sound, office |
| 55–62 s | Close | Check, final card, CTA |

Use this as a skeleton, not a straitjacket. Keep the total shot count and rhythm.

## Adapting to 9:16

- Rebuild composition; do not crop the 16:9 master.
- Put the subject in the upper 60%; captions in the lower 25%.
- Reduce text to ≤ 10 CJK chars per line.
- Replace wide group shots with one hero character or a tighter two-shot.
- Keep the same VO and music; re-time cuts only if a beat becomes unreadable.

## Production models

| Model | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Single 60 s explainer | Launch, homepage, pitch | Highest customization |
| 4–6 episode series | Creator / brand | Template reuse, lower cost per episode |
| Template license | Agency / internal team | No custom animation, faster |
| Localization pack | Global campaign | Same visuals, new VO/captions |

## What to quote

Quote by scope, not by “a video”:

- Script + storyboard.
- AI plate generation and selection.
- Character/overlay animation.
- VO, music, SFX, mix.
- Master + 2 cutdowns + 1 language.
- Extra languages / aspect ratios / revisions.

Always separate **AI generation cost** from **labor**. Log every paid call and its actual charge.

## Failure modes

- Too many ideas in 60 s → one thesis, one metaphor.
- AI plate contains people/text/UI → discard or cover; do not ship.
- Text pasted on an angled surface → replace surface with a flat panel or move text.
- Character floating above chair → align body bottom to seat; no legs if using torso style.
- Shoe/leg seam → end pant leg inside shoe; sole must cover the cap.
- Phone screen facing audience → draw the back of the phone.
- All shots same width → one close-up, one wide, one detail.
- Captions too long → split by phrase, not by character count alone.
- Music too loud → duck under VO and check −14 LUFS.
